# Full Findings

**Corpus:** ~600 posts over 30 days from one active news/OSINT account (~1.3k followers).
**Comparison set:** several large "pro-poster" accounts (hundreds of thousands of followers) for single-post benchmarking.
**Score:** `likes + 2·reposts + 1.5·replies + 2·quotes`.

## 1. Roots vs replies
- Root posts averaged materially higher engagement than replies (~7× on likes).
- Implication: the opening post carries the thread. Put your strongest, most self-contained idea first.

## 2. Topic signal
Average score by topic (sample sizes small for some — treated as advisory):
- Hard geopolitical/war items and named political events scored highest.
- Market and general-analysis items scored in the middle.
- Niche/low-context items scored lowest.
- **Caveat:** thin-sample topics are advisory only, not conclusions.

## 3. Timing
- Two clear daily peaks emerged in the account's timezone: a morning peak and a mid-afternoon peak.
- A mid-morning lull and an early-afternoon lull consistently underperformed.
- **Method note:** all timestamps analyzed in UTC, then converted to the account's local zone.

## 4. The standalone-winner shape (the core finding)
Linking replies to their root revealed a small set of replies that *beat their own opening post*. They shared three traits:
1. **Colored-square + ALL-CAPS named-concept opener** (the openers they beat used soft prose).
2. **They name a concept** — a repeatable mental handle — rather than just report a fact.
3. **Standalone completeness:** headline → 1–2 bare receipt lines → a sharp turn; readable cold.

By opener type (reply engagement):
- square opener: ~0.57 avg likes, liked ≥1 on ~46%
- square + CAPS: ~0.42 avg likes, ~33%
- prose: ~0.24 avg likes, ~19%

Squares roughly doubled prose. ~88% of square replies carried a named CAPS concept.

## 5. Single-post masters (benchmark)
- Pure text (no link-card) outperformed link-card posts for raw likes.
- Winning shape: named actor + present-tense verb + stakes, one breath, no preamble.
- Plain openers beat "BREAKING/URGENT"-labeled ones.
- Winners used nearly the full character budget.
- None ended on a question mark.

## 6. What kills a feed
- Product announcements, self-promo, and meta posts scored near zero and depress the surrounding feed.
- Keep the feed to substance; route promo elsewhere.

## Synthesis
The highest-leverage single post is: **one self-contained, named, present-tense, high-stakes claim, near the full character budget, no click-away, no question mark, authoritative.** Coining a *named concept* turns a hot take into a reusable idea readers repeat.
